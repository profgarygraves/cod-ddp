Photography & Drones

Photographers and drone operators create images for advertising, real estate, events, publications, and the web. You'll learn the camera, the digital darkroom, commercial lighting and editing, and aerial imaging, from Joshua Tree to the studio.

Careers: Commercial photographer, drone pilot, photo editor

What you'll learn

The knowledge, skills, and abilities each program builds. Open a program to see its list.

CA

Photography

Knowledge

What you'll understand

  • Exposure, light, and composition
  • The history of photography
  • Commercial photography standards for web, ads, and print
  • File management and output

Skills

What you'll be able to do

  • Shoot with a DSLR in manual mode
  • Run a digital darkroom workflow: import, rank, edit, publish, archive
  • Retouch and composite in Photoshop
  • Shoot and process black and white film
  • Lay out images for publication

Abilities

How you'll use it on the job

  • Plan and deliver client photo shoots
  • Price photography work and manage clients
  • Curate a professional photography portfolio
CA

Commercial Drone Imaging

Knowledge

What you'll understand

  • Drone imaging and an overview of FAA rules
  • Geographic information systems (GIS) and mapping
  • How construction and public safety teams use aerial imaging

Skills

What you'll be able to do

  • Capture aerial photo and video
  • Edit, color, and manage drone imagery
  • Edit video in Premiere Pro
  • Shoot commercial-quality photographs

Abilities

How you'll use it on the job

  • Deliver aerial images for real estate, construction, agriculture, and public safety
  • Prepare to earn the FAA Part 107 Remote Pilot Certificate on your own
  • Gain field experience through work experience

Careers

Photographers and drone operators make images for clients: real estate listings, weddings and events, products, food, and advertising. Drone work adds aerial photo, video, and mapping for real estate, construction, agriculture, and inspections.

"Typical entry education" is what most people in the job have. Creative employers hire from portfolios, so a COD certificate plus strong work can start your career, and a transfer degree keeps the bachelor's option open. About 2 in 3 photographers are self-employed, so business skills matter as much as camera skills.

Career ladder

  1. Start

    Photo assistant, photo editor, event photographer

  2. Grow

    Commercial, real estate, or wedding photographer; licensed drone pilot

  3. Lead

    Studio owner, aerial imaging business, photo director

Where the jobs are

  • Real estate and vacation rental marketing
  • Weddings, resorts, and events
  • Construction, solar, and agriculture (drone mapping and inspection)
  • Advertising, product, and food photography
  • News, publishing, and tourism
  • Your own photography business

Skills employers want

  • Camera, exposure, and lighting
  • Editing and asset management (digital darkroom)
  • Drone flight and aerial imaging
  • FAA Part 107 Remote Pilot Certificate: required to fly drones for pay
  • Pricing, contracts, and client service (DDP 195)
  • A portfolio for each kind of work you want
  • Find jobs on HandshakeJobs and internships for COD students. Sign in with your COD account.
  • DDP 195Business practices and portfolio preparation: freelancing, pricing, clients, and your portfolio.
  • DDP 095AEarn units for a design job or internship.
  • Career centerCareer counseling, resumes, and job fairs at COD.

Transfer
