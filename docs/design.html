Graphic Design & Branding

Graphic designers turn ideas into logos, posters, packaging, ads, publications, and social content. You'll learn the design process from research and sketches to finished work, on the Adobe tools studios use, and you'll leave with a portfolio.

Careers: Graphic designer, brand designer, marketing designer, art director

    DDP 100 has no prerequisite. Bring your curiosity; your instructors will teach you the tools.

    Most 3-unit DDP courses include 54 hours of hands-on lab time, so you build real projects from week one.

What you'll learn

The knowledge, skills, and abilities each program builds. Open a program to see its list.

AADesign Studies

Knowledge

What you'll understand

  • Design principles: composition, color, hierarchy, and visual communication
  • Art history from prehistory to the present
  • Typography: letterforms, type history, and type as image
  • User experience research methods
  • Programming fundamentals in Python

Skills

What you'll be able to do

  • Take a project from research and thumbnails to finished design
  • Build advertising campaigns and design systems
  • Create typographic solutions for print and screen
  • Prototype and usability-test an app or website
  • Produce video, motion graphics, or 3D work (your choice)

Abilities

How you'll use it on the job

  • Explain and defend design decisions in critique
  • Collaborate on a creative team
  • Assemble a portfolio for transfer to a four-year design program
AAGraphic Design & Marketing

Knowledge

What you'll understand

  • How design supports branding, marketing, and business goals
  • Typography and layout for print and digital
  • Design for social causes and calls to action
  • User experience principles
  • Business fundamentals that clients care about

Skills

What you'll be able to do

  • Vector illustration and logo design in Illustrator
  • Multipage layout and publication design in InDesign
  • Advertising campaigns across formats
  • Research, ideate, and prototype design solutions

Abilities

How you'll use it on the job

  • Translate a client's goals into a visual strategy
  • Work in a real design job through work experience
  • Prepare to transfer into a design or marketing bachelor's program
CAGraphic Design

Knowledge

What you'll understand

  • The history of graphic design and its major movements
  • Typography, grids, and visual systems
  • Print production: paper, ink, color, and file preparation
  • Packaging, sustainability, and materials
  • Social media and user experience design

Skills

What you'll be able to do

  • Professional work in Photoshop, Illustrator, and InDesign
  • Advertising campaigns and brand systems
  • Package design with dielines and 3D mockups
  • Web pages and social content
  • Generative AI tools for ideation and production

Abilities

How you'll use it on the job

  • Manage a design project from brief to final files
  • Run the business side of design: estimates, billing, and clients
  • Present a professional portfolio to employers
CADigital Design & Production

Knowledge

What you'll understand

  • Design fundamentals and the design process
  • Image formats, resolution, and color for print, web, and video
  • Photography basics: exposure and composition

Skills

What you'll be able to do

  • Photoshop, Illustrator, and InDesign
  • Video editing in Premiere Pro and motion graphics in After Effects
  • Digital photography
  • Preparing for the Adobe Certified Professional exams

Abilities

How you'll use it on the job

  • Create content across print, web, and video
  • Select and present your best work in an online portfolio

Careers

Designers plan and build the visual side of a brand: logos, ads, packaging, signs, social posts, publications, and websites. A typical week mixes client meetings, sketching, building files in Adobe tools, and revising work after feedback.

"Typical entry education" is what most people in the job have. Creative employers hire from portfolios, so a COD certificate plus strong work can start your career, and a transfer degree keeps the bachelor's option open. About 1 in 5 graphic designers and 3 in 5 art directors are self-employed.

Career ladder

  1. Start

    Production artist, junior designer, marketing coordinator

  2. Grow

    Graphic designer, brand designer, packaging designer

  3. Lead

    Senior designer, art director, creative director

Where the jobs are

  • In-house marketing teams at resorts, hotels, and golf clubs
  • Advertising and design agencies
  • Music festivals, events, and nonprofits
  • Print shops, sign makers, and packaging companies
  • Cities, colleges, and health systems
  • Your own studio or freelance clients

Skills employers want

  • Photoshop, Illustrator, and InDesign
  • Typography, layout, and color
  • Brand systems and style guides
  • Presenting ideas and taking feedback
  • Adobe Certified Professional exams
  • A portfolio of finished projects

Transfer

The Design Studies AA and the Graphic Design & Marketing AA are built for transfer, using Cal-GETC general education.

20 of the 21 DDP courses on this path transfer to the CSU, and 8 also transfer to the UC. Each university decides which courses count toward its major, so check ASSIST.org and meet with a counselor.
