How noncredit works

Noncredit classes have no tuition and no units. They are graded pass or no pass, and you can repeat them. Each Certificate of Completion pairs an A class, which teaches the basics, with a B class, which adds practice exams and portfolio projects for the Adobe Certified exam.

Noncredit classes use a separate, shorter application. Register for noncredit at COD.

Ready for more? Credit courses like DDP 101 (Photoshop), DDP 102 (Illustrator), and DDP 120 (Premiere Pro) count toward degrees and certificates.

"Typical entry education" is what most people in the job have. Creative employers hire from portfolios, so a COD certificate plus strong work can start your career, and a transfer degree keeps the bachelor's option open. Desktop publishing on its own is shrinking. Pair software skills with design, video, or marketing for the strongest job prospects.

Career ladder

  1. Start

  2. Grow

Transfer

Noncredit classes do not earn transfer credit. When you are ready, credit courses like DDP 101 and DDP 102 count toward degrees and transfer to the CSU.
