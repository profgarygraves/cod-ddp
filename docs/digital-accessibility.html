Accessibility

The only California community college offering this certificate. Can be completed online.

Every college, government agency, and large company must make its websites and documents usable by people with disabilities. Accessibility specialists know the law, fix inaccessible content, and match people with assistive technology.

Careers: Accessibility specialist, assistive technology specialist, document remediation specialist

Foundations for Accessibility

Knowledge

What you'll understand

  • Accessibility law: Section 504, Section 508, and the ADA
  • Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG)
  • The history and foundations of disability access
  • Assistive technologies and how people use them

Skills

What you'll be able to do

  • Create accessible Word, PowerPoint, and PDF documents
  • Fix (remediate) documents that are not accessible
  • Evaluate content against accessibility standards
  • Match assistive technology to a person's needs

Abilities

How you'll use it on the job

  • Advise an organization on accessibility compliance
  • Train coworkers to create accessible content
  • Support students and employees with disabilities

Careers

Accessibility specialists make sure websites, documents, and courses work for people with disabilities. They test pages, fix inaccessible files, train staff, and match people with assistive technology. Colleges, governments, and large companies are required to do this work.

Creative employers hire from portfolios, so a COD certificate plus strong work can start your career, and a transfer degree keeps the bachelor's option open. There is no single government job category for accessibility, so the pay data shows the closest related jobs.

Career ladder

  1. Start

    Accessibility tester, document remediation specialist, alternate media assistant

  2. Grow

    Accessibility specialist or coordinator, assistive technology specialist

  3. Lead

    Accessibility manager, compliance lead, consultant

Where the jobs are

  • Colleges and school districts
  • City, county, state, and tribal governments
  • Health systems
  • Banks, retailers, and tech companies
  • Web agencies
  • Consulting and freelance remediation

Skills employers want

  • Section 504, Section 508, ADA, and WCAG
  • Accessible Word, PowerPoint, and PDF files
  • Screen readers and other assistive technology
  • Testing websites for accessibility
  • IAAP CPACC certification (optional, widely recognized)
  • Clear writing and training others

Transfer

3 of the 3 DDP courses on this path transfer to the CSU, and 1 also transfer to the UC. Each university decides which courses count toward its major, so check ASSIST.org and meet with a counselor.
